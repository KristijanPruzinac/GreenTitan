# GreenTitan hardware

The physical mower: mechanics, electronics, sensors, the phone, CAD and print rules, and what the real world does to all of it. The software is in `ARCHITECTURE.md`, and the doc rules are in `../CLAUDE.md`.

## 1. Mechanical baseline: Fusion "Mower V3"

- **Source of truth:** the Fusion 360 cloud design **Mower V3** in project "GreenTitan Mower". Save milestones as new *versions* of Mower V3. Don't create "V4" files. Milestones so far: v25 (drive bracket/gearbox fit) and v26 (Blender cover, later rejected).
- The frame is four 2020 aluminium extrusions with printed parts.
- **Front = the big drive-wheel end.** The caster(s) are at the rear.
- Two driven wheels (differential drive) and two rear **swivel casters** (wheel r 30.6 mm trailing 34.4 mm behind the swivel axis). The wheels are **plastic**, so expect slip on grass.
- Blade motor: MY1016-type DC motor, switched by a relay.
- The GNSS antenna sits directly **above the blade motor**, at Fusion world (61.8, 266.1). The software treats that point as `base_link`.
- Battery: Einhell 18 V Power X-Change pack in a printed holder. The Einhell adapter is the mating surface: it mounts under the battery with its rails along X. The battery model only marks the clearance envelope.
- Being replaced by a clean-slate redesign (2026-11-09, §6).

## 2. Drive train

History: the original 23HE30-2804S NEMA23 steppers in direct drive **didn't have enough torque for real terrain**. That was the first field test, before 2026-10.

Current design (2026-10-26):
- **Motors:** 2× **23HS32-4004S** (NEMA23, 82 mm long, Ø38.1×1.6 boss, 4×Ø5.1 holes on 47.14, Ø8 shaft 21 mm with a 15 mm D-cut). The shafts stay as bought: behind the 9 mm wall, 12 mm of shaft is in the gearbox. The user has three of these, taken from their CNC.
- **Gearbox:** 2× StepperOnline **MG23-G10-D8**, 10:1, 55.5 mm long.
  - Input: round Ø57, 4× M4 threaded holes (10 deep) on 47.14, Ø38.1×3 recess, 8 mm bore for short shafts (9.5–13.5 mm).
  - Output: 8 mm thick square 57 flange, 4×Ø5.2 holes on 47.14, Ø40×3 pilot, **Ø14×34 keyed shaft**.
- **Holder (NemaMotorBracket):**
  - The end plates are 9 mm walls between motor and gearbox, giving 12 mm of shaft engagement. M4×20 screws go in from the motor side.
  - The motors drop in from the open bottom, because there's no room to slide them in axially. The gearbox slides on from outside afterwards.
  - The motor leads point up through 18×23 openings. Side windows give hex-key access to the upper M4 screws.
  - The wheels moved out 54.5 mm per side, so **WHEEL_BASE in firmware is outdated**.
- **Drivers:** 2× **DM542T** (STEP/DIR).
  - Check the supply voltage: DM542T drivers are typically rated about 20–50 VDC, and an 18 V pack runs about 16–20 V. Torque at speed also drops at low voltage.
  - The microstep DIP setting must match the firmware's steps/rev.
- **Speed budget:** STEP comes from the ESP32 LEDC hardware since 2026-10-27 (ARCHITECTURE Known issue #1), so there's no firmware step-rate cap. At full step, 0.3 m/s is ~1275 steps/s (≈380 motor rpm), and the 0.5 m/s limit is ~2125 steps/s (≈640 rpm), where the 23HS32's torque is well down. Line following cruises at 0.3 m/s since 2026-10-27 (ARCHITECTURE §6).
- StepperOnline STEP models download from `files.omc-stepperonline.com/<model>.STEP`.

## 5. Android phone ("robot brain")

- **Device:** Samsung Galaxy A12 on Android 12, stripped and frozen with `tools/android/` (debloat, freeze OTA, Termux, NetGuard firewall, IP-camera app). The repo must eventually let anyone set up an old Android phone for GreenTitan. Instructions live in `tools/android/README.md`.
- **Sensors:**
  - accelerometer: useful for tilt, bumps and stuck detection
  - rear camera, which looks **forward**, and the front camera
  - **no gyro**, so the MPU6050 stays the yaw-rate source
  - phone GPS isn't used, because the RTK receiver is far better
- **Mounting:** the phone isn't waterproof, so it sits in a **wet compartment that drains outside** and never opens into the electronics bay. The camera windows are drip-proof but unobstructed. Exo concept (§6, **proposed** 2026-11-10): on its long side in a pocket of the front fascia, the back cameras looking forward through an open window under the lid's eave, the pocket open downward. It goes in from above with the front lid off. The OTG adapter plugs into the phone's end, and the two need about 210 mm side by side. That is more than the 192 mm between the front frame posts, so the pocket sits ahead of the frame. The front camera sees nothing there. The plan before it was a "sail" on the cover with windows for both cameras.
- **Link** (decided 2026-11-10): a small 2-in-1 USB-C OTG adapter plugs into the phone; the ESP32 goes on its USB-A data port (USB-A to USB-C cable), and the 5 V wires from the buck feed its charge port through a USB-C plug with a 2-pin screw terminal. The owned Delock 64298 hub does the same job but is bulkier (100 mm, 15 cm lead); it stays as the spare.
- **Open software questions** (ARCHITECTURE §15): running ROS 2 on unrooted Android, USB-serial access from Termux, and a camera pipeline. Expect detection-class vision (TFLite) to be feasible on the Helio P35. Real-time visual SLAM is marginal.

## 6. CAD, printing and cover rules

Printing:
- Everything prints on an **Anycubic Kobra X**. The bed is 260×260; plan on about 240 mm usable. 234×200 was accepted, 240×200 was rejected.
- **No flat plates.** Add ribs, flanges or seams for stiffness. Use as little plastic as possible.
- Leave about **5 mm clearance around bought, mass-produced parts**, because batches vary and the CAD models are approximate. NormalFit (0.128 mm) is only for printed-to-printed fits. 2020 extrusion gets 0 clearance.
- Colour only as horizontal layer changes (flat z-planes) or as separate parts. Big parts like the cover print in one piece.
- Solve fit problems inside the existing printed part instead of adding parts. No spacers, no T-nuts, no "visor glass" the user doesn't own.

Cover (in progress):
- One-piece and rain-proof from above.
- Style decided 2026-10-26: "no curves, all wedgy and chamfered, techno". The inspiration is U-Boat Worx subs.
- Built natively in Fusion as planar B-rep. Two attempts designed in Blender/SDF were rejected on looks, so agree on the direction before modelling.
- Requirements: the phone sail and wet compartment, a radome hatch for the GNSS patch above the blade motor, and the UHF whip inside.

Clean-slate redesign (2026-11-09): the mechanical design starts from scratch. The old studies in `cad/` and `outputs/` (roof_reset, canopy, cowl, spine_b, packaging_b_review) and the Fusion scripts were removed. Only these parts carry over:
- **Frame base:** 2020 aluminium extrusion (lengths free), 2020 corner brackets (90° owned now, any angle allowed), 2020 drop-in T-nuts and screws.
- **Electronics and drive:** ESP32 devboard, C94-M8P with its UHF whip, ANN-MS patch antenna (5 m RG174, Ø100 ground plane), MPU6050, 2× VL53L8CX, 2× 23HS32-4004S + 2× MG23-G10-D8 (interfaces in §2), 2× DM542T, MY1016 blade motor, single-channel relay module, waterproof buck → 5 V, Einhell 18 V pack + adapter, Galaxy A12 (§3, §5).
- **Casters:** the two existing swivel casters (§2), reused at the rear (2026-11-10).
- Not carried over: wheels, blade and holder, cover, all printed parts.

Layout decisions (2026-11-10):
- Drive wheels and phone at the front, two casters at the rear. A centred layout is out: the two stepper + gearbox stacks (137.5 mm each) fill the axle line.
- The body gets wider and shorter than V3, with the casters spread further apart.
- The blade tucks under the drive steppers as far as it can, which needs bigger drive wheels.
- Each mechanical group has its own chamber: the blade chamber is closed off from the steppers, and both from the electronics.
- The mower is a module. Deck, blade and blade motor (in a well) bolt on from below as one part. Without it the frame is a general base with a roomy, flat electronics bay.

Exo (**proposed**, 2026-11-10): the model is `cad/exo/`, written in CadQuery (Python): the frame, every bought part as a plain solid at its size and place, and the twelve printed parts with their ribs, seats and screw holes. No wiring. Every shared number is in `dims.py`, which also lists under `ASSUMED` the sizes taken for parts that are not measured yet. `python build.py` builds the model and checks it: nothing runs through anything else; every printed part fits a 245 mm bed and prints without supports (flat bridges up to 30 mm); every part that is taken off comes off in a straight line past its neighbours; every frame bolt sits on a free slot with plastic under its head. It also lists the fasteners, the size, the mass and the balance. `--export` writes STEP and STL files and each printed part as it lies on the bed (`out/`, not in the repo); `view.py` renders them in Blender. `cad/concepts/exo.py` is the Blender layout study the model was built from; it is not kept in step.
- **Frame:** a plain box of 2020 on 18 brackets of 90°. 13 lengths, 3.23 m: 4× 425 (rails), 4× 105 (posts), 4× 192 (cross beams), 1× 340 (tail beam). Bottom ring 70–90 mm up, top ring 195–215 mm, 232 mm wide over the rails.
- **Size:** 503 mm long, 385 mm wide over the wheels (V3: ~402 mm), 240 mm high, track 328 mm (V3: 364.7 mm). Drive wheels Ø240 × 56, the largest that print in one piece; the axle is 120 mm up, and the wheels reach 11 mm ahead of the fascia's skin, as far as the ToF mounts. Ø240 stays (decided 2026-11-10): the gearbox sits 1.5 mm over the bottom rail, so smaller wheels (about Ø200) would mean ending the bottom rails behind the axle. Rear swing radius about 0.41 m (V3: 0.415 m of body behind the axle).
- **Drive:** as on V3 (§2): stepper inside, gearbox outside, 9 mm of printed plastic between them, so the 21 mm shaft has 12 mm in the gearbox (its bore takes 9.5–13.5). The steppers lie tail to tail, 10 mm apart, so neither can be put in from inside the tub. Each drive is a unit that is screwed together on the bench: stepper, a printed drive plate (83 × 91 × 9 mm) and gearbox, held by four M4×20 from the motor side. The unit goes in from outside: the stepper passes between the bottom and the top rail and through a window in the front tub's side wall, and the plate lands in a pocket in that wall. Four M4×25 hold it there, their heads beside the gearbox, their nyloc nuts held in hex pockets inside the tub. The wall is 18 mm thick at the drive and bolts to the bottom and the top rail with four M5. The unit comes out the same way once the wheel is off; no screw of the drive has to be reached inside the tub. No bought part is cut or machined (rules below). The wheel is one piece on the bought flange hub, half over the gearbox: a 3 mm web on its outside face, 24 chevron lugs, three M5 through the hub's flange.
- **Blade:** cut Ø270 (2026-11-10), centre 168 mm behind the axle (V3: 180), just clear of the drive wheels: anything wider than the 272 mm between the wheels has to sit behind them. About 42 m/s at the tip at 3000 rpm, blade about 55 mm above the ground and the skirt's edge 50 mm (V3: blade 61–73; higher doesn't fit, the deck's top is at 70 mm under the rails), uncut strip along a wall about 7 cm, as on V3. The deck bell is wider than the bed (Ø302 at the skirt), so it is two equal halves, 5 mm thick, split across the mower under the motor. Each half prints standing on its cut edge, diagonally on the bed, and the skirt flares at 45° so it needs no supports that way. The halves are the same part. Each bolts into the bottom rails from below with four M5, and they are joined by the rails, an M4 through lugs at each end of the joint, two M4 through flanges beside the motor, and the flange of the 57BLR90, which stands on both and reaches up through a collared hole in the rear tub's floor. The blade disc is printed, Ø220 × 4 with ribs on top; it sits on a flange coupling and carries three pivoting razor blades. A Ø280 cut needs the motor about 6 mm further back, where the carrier board is: it only fits with a carrier board of at most about 49 mm (drawn: 55).
- **Casters:** the 2 inch plate casters (under review below) under the corners of rail and tail beam, swivel axis at x −328, y ±133 mm. Three of the four plate bolts land in slots: two in the tail beam, one in the rail. Their 72 mm height puts the plate at the frame's underside; the 3 inch ones would need a mount 105 mm up.
- **Printed parts:** 12 (9 different), no supports, 4.2 kg as solid plastic. Green: front tub 192×182×141 (673 g) and rear tub 192×221×122 (571 g), floor down; front lid 240×236×21 (397 g) and rear lid 240×241×16 (315 g), top down; fascia 238×50, 151 high, standing on its bottom edge (255 g). Black: 2 deck halves, the same part, standing on the cut face, diagonal: 228×228 on the bed, 151 high (247 g each); blade disc Ø220×6 (204 g); 2 drive plates, the same part, 83×91×9 lying flat (72 g each); 2 wheels Ø240×56, outside face down (572 g each; the right one is the mirror image). Nothing is a flat plate but the drive plates, which are clamped between stepper and gearbox: the tubs and lids have ribs and rims, the fascia its side plates and the phone pocket, the disc ribs on top. The longest bridge is 27 mm, over the battery strap.
- **Rain:** the tubs hang between the rails with their rims 1 mm under the top ring. The lids lie on the top ring, their rims hanging 10 mm down outside the rails. The front lid is 5 mm higher and laps 14 mm over the rear lid, above the middle cross beam; under the lap a drip rib hangs into a gutter in the rear lid that runs out at both sides. At the front the lid ends in an eave 13 mm ahead of the fascia. The holes in the tubs: cable notches in the rims (under the lids), the stepper windows (closed by the drive plates, which lie in their pockets under the top rails), the e-stop, and the blade motor's hole in the rear tub's floor with a collar 15 mm high round it. The antenna coax goes down behind the rear top beam, inside the lid's rim, and in over the beam in a groove. Open to the weather are only the camera window and the underside of the phone pocket, both outside the tubs.
- **Fastening:** every printed part bolts to the 2020 with M5 button-head screws into drop-in T-nuts, 41 in all (count the owned T-nuts): front tub 12 (8 of them through the drive walls into the bottom and top rails), rear tub 7, deck halves 4 each, lids 4 each, fascia 6. `build.py` prints the whole fastener list. The lids come off upward, four screws each, and then everything inside lifts out; the tubs and the deck come off downward, the fascia forward, the drive units sideways.
- **Layout:** front tub: the steppers, the two DM542T upright side by side behind them (on two rails, M4 self-tapping screws from above), the buck ahead of them on a rest with a cable tie. Rear tub: the blade motor in its collar, the BLD-510B along the right wall, the C94 upright on the left wall with the whip folded flat under the lid, the carrier board with the ESP32 and the IMU on four posts behind the motor (at most 55 × 100 mm), the battery on its side across the tail, on ribs with a strap round it, the e-stop through the rear wall. To take the battery out: lift it 9 mm over its stops, tilt it and pull it forward (its rear 12 mm sit under the top beam). Fascia: the phone pocket (§5), the ToF sensors at the upper corners crossed 45°, 188 mm up, on mounts that stand 11 mm proud of the fascia (a sunk pocket would clip the 45° field) and stay above the camera window, and the charge contact in the middle, 192 mm up under the lid's eave (2026-11-10: at 80–90 mm they sat in the uncut grass ahead of the nose). The contact sits in a boss on the outside, so nothing stiff reaches into the phone's way; its leads run down the 4.5 mm between the phone's back and the skin. The camera window is centred 70 mm off the centreline; the phone with the OTG adapter at its end leaves no room to centre it. The GNSS antenna sits on its ground plane on the rear lid, above the blade motor; its coax is coiled on the battery.
- **Where it departs from the layout decisions above and the rules below (to decide):** the body is narrower than V3, not wider: at the first Exo model's width (track 444 mm) no lid or tub fits the bed in one piece. The stepper drivers and the buck share the front tub with the steppers; no shaft turns inside a tub, and the drive plates close the windows in its walls. The battery and the blade driver sit in the rear tub, not in the front half: the mower has no support ahead of the axle, and the battery is the one heavy part that is free to move. The fascia bolts to the outer faces of the front posts (two M5 a side) and with two long M5 low into the bottom cross beam, not to the top beam: anything bolted up there would sit in the phone's way in. The drive plate bolts to the tub's wall, not to the 2020: a plastic-to-plastic joint that carries the wheel's load, against the rule below. With the plate as part of the wall, the second stepper can't be put in without cutting its shaft or slotting the wall open (Drive).
- **Mass and balance (from the model; no cables or screws, printed parts as solid plastic):** 14.5 kg (2020 and brackets 1.7, printed 4.2, bought 8.5). Centre of mass 93 mm behind the axle and 126 mm up: 26 % of the weight on the casters (V3: 24 %), and the mower tips onto its nose at about 36° (V3: about 38°). From the layout study: with the battery in the front tub 66 mm and about 28°; with the axle 60 mm further back in the body, as it was at first, 52 mm and about 22°.
- **Firmware and sim numbers that change with it:** `WHEEL_RADIUS` about 0.12 and `WHEEL_BASE` 0.328 (V3: 0.0749 and 0.3647), `GNSS_ANTENNA_BEHIND_AXLE` 0.168 and `GNSS_ANTENNA_HEIGHT` about 0.23 (0.180 and 0.28), the charge contact 0.118 m ahead of the axle and 0.192 m up (the docking firmware assumes 0.12 ahead), `TOF_MOUNT_X` 0.115, `TOF_MOUNT_Y` 0.098 and `TOF_MOUNT_Z` 0.188 (0.11, 0.15 and 0.09), `IMU_X_BEHIND_AXLE` about 0.23 (V3: 0), the coverage row spacing for the Ø270 cut, the caster positions in the sim.

Buildability rules for the redesign (2026-11-10):
- **Bought parts go in as bought** (2026-11-10): the mower is meant to be rebuilt by hobbyists with a printer and hand tools. Nothing bought is cut, ground, drilled or machined (no shortened shafts, no CNC or lathe work); the only cut is 2020 to length. A design that needs more than that is wrong, however well it fits.
- **Structure is metal only:** 2020 extrusion and bought brackets. The cage becomes a plain box on 90° brackets. Printed parts are skins and holders that bolt to the extrusion; plastic-to-plastic joints only connect, they never carry load.
- **Casters and wheels bolt to metal:** caster plates onto a 2020 beam, wheels over a metal flange hub on the gearbox shaft.
- **Printed parts:** at most 245×245 mm, no supports, green or black only, a colour change only by layer. No decals, lettering, light bars or other parts that can't be made.
- **Rain:** panels overlap like roof tiles, the upper over the lower, with the seams raised and over a frame beam. No gaskets.
- **Charging port at the front** (the mower docks nose first). Power parts (battery, drivers, steppers) in the front half, the ESP32 and GNSS at the rear.
- **The gyro stays on the ESP32** (I2C or SPI), not on USB to the phone. The IMU sits on the carrier board (decided 2026-11-10), not on a cable at the axle midpoint as on V3 (§2). Its position doesn't change the yaw rate; `IMU_X_BEHIND_AXLE` takes the spin's centripetal part out of the tilt estimate.
- **Wiring:** plug connectors throughout, XT60/XT30 for power. No soldered signal joints.
- New parts come from AliExpress or an EU shop that sells to private buyers, and the user approves each one first.

Parts under review (**proposed**, 2026-11-10; read from AliExpress listings, nothing chosen or ordered; prices converted from the listing currency and rounded; budget for all new parts about €200):
- **Battery** (must be ≥ 72 Wh, the Einhell pack's capacity; the Einhell 18 V is below the DM542T minimum and can't be monitored): generic 7S3P 18650 pack, 25.9 V nominal / 29.4 V full, built-in BMS, 29.4 V 2 A charger included, 133×100×67 mm, about €45. The listed "40 Ah" is false; 21 cells give roughly 100–190 Wh depending on the cells. The pack has no data port. Listing variants (2026-11-10): an XT60 discharge lead plus a DC-jack charge lead and the charger; the BMS current is not stated, and the mower draws up to about 20 A (blade 14 A rated).
- **Battery monitoring** (on the carrier board): an INA226 module (I2C, 36 V, about €2–3) with its 0.1 Ω shunt replaced by a 2 mΩ one (the stock shunt only measures to 0.8 A). It gives pack voltage and current. Charge state = counted current, set to 100 % when the charger tapers off at 29.4 V, with the voltage as a cross-check. Go home well above the BMS cut-off (about 19–21 V) so the BMS never cuts in normal use. If power is lost anyway: the ESP32 stores the last battery state and a reason code in NVS when the voltage collapses (a large capacitor on the 5 V rail holds it for that long) and reports its reset reason at the next boot; the phone has its own battery, sees the link drop and keeps the last battery values, time and position.
- **Blade unit**, candidates (the disc is printed, bolted to a metal flange coupling, with three pivoting robot-mower razor blades; about Ø250 cut at 3000 rpm is about 39 m/s at the tip, the speed range of commercial robot mowers):
  - **Owned, first choice:** StepperOnline 57BLR90-24-01 brushless motor (24 V, 220 W, 3500 rpm, Ø57×89 mm) with the BLD-510B driver (12–48 V, 15 A). No purchase besides the flange coupling (about €2) and the razor blades. Run throttled to about 3000 rpm. To check: shaft diameter and the driver's speed input.
  - 57BL industrial brushless motor (to buy), 24 V 3000 rpm, Hall sensors, 57 mm flange, 8 mm shaft: 60 W about €21, 100 W about €27, 150 W about €31. Driver: generic 6–60 V 400 W Hall board with PWM, direction and brake inputs (ZS-X11H type), about €6–12; the seller's matched driver costs about €30 more. 8 mm flange coupling about €2. Quiet, speed-controlled, no brushes.
  - Brushless cordless-mower motor with Hall sensors: 12–30 V, 2900 rpm no-load at 24 V, 1.7 kg, about €22 (the seller notes the price is negotiable, so unreliable). Same kind of driver. The blade mount on its shaft is unknown.
  - Brushed 775/795 motors and kits with a 200×30×1.5 mm bar blade: about €7–38, 5 mm shaft. 10 000–20 000 rpm no-load: over 100 m/s at the tip of a 200 mm bar, loud, and only a 200 mm cut. Would have to run throttled.
  - The owned MY1016 (24 V brushed, about 2 kg): costs nothing and runs at its rated voltage on the 7S pack. Heavy and tall.

- **Casters:** the current ones mount on an M10 stem, which doesn't bolt to 2020 and is hard to tighten. The label on the unit is still to be read. Run signal therefore (**proposed** 2026-11-10): SV, not EN. The manual: SV takes 0–5 V or 5 V PWM at 1–2 kHz, and below 0.3 V the motor stops. A 1 kΩ resistor across SV and GND in the driver's terminal block holds SV at 0 V if the wire breaks or is unplugged (the manual's external pot is 10–50 kΩ, so the input draws well under 0.1 mA and 1 kΩ keeps it far below 0.3 V). The manual gives the supply as 24–48 V in one place and 12–48 V in another; the pack is 21–29.4 V. Cable: 2 wires, SV and GND.

- **No USB-UART adapter on the mower** (decided 2026-11-10): the ESP32 is programmed through the phone, over the air: the PC reaches the phone over Wi-Fi and the phone flashes the ESP32 over the USB cable that is already there (the devboard's USB chip puts it into the bootloader). - **Charging path** (**proposed** 2026-11-10): charger (29.4 V 2 A, comes with the pack) → a DC socket matching its barrel plug (size known when it is here) → station half of the pogo pair (leads soldered; the part has only solder pins; centre pin +, shell −) → mower half → ideal diode module → battery + between the shunt and the e-stop.
  - Order on the + line: pack → 2 mΩ shunt → charge joins → e-stop → loads. The INA226 then counts charge and discharge, and a pressed e-stop doesn't stop charging.
  - This charges through the pack's discharge lead. The listings show a second DC-jack charge lead and don't say whether the BMS charges through both. If it doesn't, the diode output goes to that charge lead and the charge current is not counted.
  - Open contacts: the station contact is live all the time at 29.4 V DC (extra-low voltage), limited to 2 A by the charger. What is left is a metal piece across the pins (the magnet pulls steel onto them; how the charger behaves into a short is unknown until it is here) and corrosion when wet under voltage, so the contact sits under a roof, facing sideways or down. The mower contact is dead off the dock (ideal diode), which the dock sense also needs. A plain Schottky diode is rejected: its reverse leakage (mA when warm) lifts the 110 kΩ dock-sense divider, and it costs about 0.4 V of charge voltage. Fallback if the live contact gives trouble: switch it in the station (reed + relay, §8) with its own magnet away from the connector, since the pogo's magnets would hold a reed closed.

Connectors on the carrier (**proposed** 2026-11-10): JST-XH for the ToF cables, pluggable screw terminal blocks for every cable that ends in bare wires (drivers 6-way, blade 2-way, 5 V in, dock sense), a shrouded 2×10 header for the ribbon, the devboard's own USB-C for the phone. ## 7. Field realities

These are what the software must survive:
- **RTK GPS:**
  - centimetre position with a fix, but noisy, with jumps and float/fix switches
  - at standstill the velocity direction is random, so heading from GPS is only valid while clearly moving (the firmware fuses it only above 3·sAcc, weighted by (sAcc/speed)², and removes the sideways motion of the antenna, which sits ~0.18 m behind the wheel axle and ~0.28 m up, with the gyro and the tilt; see ARCHITECTURE §5)
  - GPS heading is the **direction of travel, not of the body**
- **Wheels:** plastic on grass. They slip, especially on slopes, wet grass and turns in place. They stall against walls, and brushing an obstacle pushes the mower off its line. **Commanded motion is not odometry.**
- **Terrain:** roughness tilts and shakes the chassis. This shows up in the gyro and accelerometer, and a 2D simulation can't reproduce it.
- **Compass:** unreliable on this robot (motors, steel, currents). Not used.
- **Weather:** rain means return home. The phone and electronics must stay dry, and the cameras must still see.
- **Ground hazards:** molehills, toys, people and pets. There is no physical obstacle sensor yet, so the cameras and the accelerometer are the candidates (the ToF sensors only look at walls beside the front, and their ground filter drops anything under ~3 cm).
